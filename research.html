Research

Precision Mechatronics

My research aims to extend classical precision engineering design principles toward modern, multidisciplinary mechatronic principles — with increasingly tight requirements on accuracy, reproducibility, throughput, and cost. Research spans system-level mechatronic architecture, precision design, dynamics, actuation, control, and metrology.

Research Themes

Precision Mechatronic Design Principles

Developing and extending design principles for precision mechatronic systems. Classical deterministic design principles — such as exact constraint design and design for stiffness — are generalized toward modern mechatronic contexts where actuation, sensing, and control are tightly coupled with the mechanical design. Key themes include accuracy, repeatability, and the management of disturbances across the system.

Mechatronic Integration & Architecture

A central focus is the mechatronic integration of mechanical systems: replacing or augmenting traditionally passive components with actively controlled elements to enhance system performance while maintaining minimal complexity. This includes piezoelectric actuation, active vibration isolation, variable stiffness mechanisms, and closed-loop position control for high-throughput motion stages.

Industrial & Application-Driven Research

Research is carried out in close collaboration with industry in the semiconductor equipment, scientific instrumentation, and medical technology domains. Through this application-driven approach, new design principles are extracted and generalized — contributing to both fundamental precision engineering knowledge and the development of next-generation mechatronic systems.
