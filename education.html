Master's Track: Mechatronic Systems Design

The Mechatronic Systems Design (MSD) track is a two-year English-language MSc programme within the Department of Mechanical Engineering. It prepares students to become specialists in designing complex mechanical systems by integrating multiple engineering disciplines — from mechanical dynamics and control theory to applied physics and software integration.

Students gain hands-on experience in advanced labs and train with both open-source and commercial design and simulation tools. The track has strong ties to companies in the semiconductor, medical devices, robotics, and offshore energy sectors.

What students learn

System Thinking & Architecture

Students learn to reason at the system level — integrating mechanical, electrical, and software disciplines into coherent mechatronic designs that meet tight performance requirements.

Precision Design & Dynamics

Core coverage of mechanical system dynamics, precision design principles, materials science, and failure detection — building a foundation for high-tech instrumentation and semiconductor equipment.

Robotics & Control

Hands-on experience with robotics, intelligent mechatronic systems, control theory, and model-based tools for optimization and performance verification.

Career outlook

Graduates find roles in semiconductor equipment (ASML, Prodrive, Philips), medical devices, robotics, offshore energy, and automotive systems. TU/e's location in Brainport Eindhoven — one of Europe's leading high-tech regions — gives students exceptional access to industry partnerships and internships.

Courses

I teach and contribute to courses within the Mechatronic Systems Design track. The complete and up-to-date list of courses I am involved in is available on the TU/e Research Portal.

Precision Mechatronic Systems

MSc · Mechatronic Systems Design

Covers design principles for precision mechatronic systems — from deterministic design and exact constraint design to integration of actuation, sensing and control. Students work with real hardware case studies from industrial applications.

Mechatronic System Design Project

MSc · Mechatronic Systems Design

Integrated design project in which student teams design, build and validate a mechatronic system. Emphasises system-level thinking and the interplay between mechanical design, actuation and control.

Student Supervision

I am happy to supervise MSc and PDEng/EngD thesis projects on topics related to my research themes — in particular, precision mechatronic design, piezoelectric actuation, variable stiffness mechanisms, and mechatronic system architecture for high-tech applications.

If you are a TU/e student interested in doing your thesis project with me, feel free to get in touch by email with a brief description of your background and interests.

Topics of interest include (but are not limited to):

Piezoelectric Actuation

Design, modelling and control of piezoelectric actuators for precision positioning — including non-linear effects such as hysteresis and creep.

Variable Stiffness Mechanisms

Design and integration of mechanisms with variable or highly adjustable stiffness for use in precision mechatronic systems.

Mechatronic System Architecture

System-level design of mechatronic systems — from requirements to hardware architecture, focusing on performance-driven design decisions.
